Anyone can join Open Source Design. There are no applications, fees, or formal requirements — just read our [Code of Conduct](/about-us/code-of-conduct/) and start wherever feels right.

## Ways to get involved

### Talk to us
The best place to start is our [community forum](https://discourse.opensourcedesign.net). Ask questions, share what you're working on, or just lurk and get a feel for the community.

### Come to events
We run and attend events throughout the year — meetups, conference devrooms, and design sprints. Browse [upcoming events](/events/) or help organise one by opening a pull request on [GitHub](https://github.com/opensourcedesign/opensourcedesign.github.io/tree/main/content/events).

### Contribute design work
Open source projects post design requests on our [job board](/jobs/). Some are paid, many are volunteer. If you have a project that needs a designer, you can [post a job](/jobs/job-form/) there too.

### Work on this website
The site is built with Hugo and contributions are welcome — from fixing a broken link to redesigning a section. Find issues and open pull requests on [GitHub](https://github.com/opensourcedesign/opensourcedesign.github.io).

### Help grow the organisation
We coordinate community work in our [organisation repository](https://github.com/opensourcedesign/organization). If you want to help shape how OSD runs, that's the place.

### Support us financially
We accept contributions through [Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/opensourcedesign/contribute) — one-off or recurring, whatever works for you. (We don't accept contributions from organisations involved in casino & gambling, pharmacy/weight-loss products, or SEO and social media buying services.)

## What membership looks like

There's no membership card or formal sign-up. You're part of the community when you participate — whether that means posting in the forum, helping someone on GitHub, attending events, or contributing financially. Involvement means whatever you want it to mean.

If you're consistently active over time and start taking on responsibility for some aspect of OSD, you may naturally become a core contributor. There's no formal process for that either — it just happens through sustained engagement.
